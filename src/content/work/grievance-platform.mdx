## The problem

Grievance handling at the Alwar plant ran entirely on paper. Workers wrote concerns on slips or logged them in departmental registers called **Red Books**. Each department kept its own book, and nothing connected them.

Once a complaint was written down, there was no way to confirm it had been received, see where it stood, or know when it was closed. HR had no plant-wide view of what the workforce was unhappy about. On the shop floor, the result was predictable: people stopped believing their complaints went anywhere.

## What I set out to build

The brief was to replace the Red Books completely, without disrupting production and without a budget. That set the constraints:

- **Zero running cost**, so the system could survive after the internship ended
- **Hindi, English and Marwadi**, because the workforce does not read one language
- **Usable at any literacy level**, including by workers who struggle to type
- **Real-time status** for the worker, and a single dashboard for HR
- **A fresh start**: old paper records were deliberately not migrated, to avoid carrying inconsistent data into the new system

## How it works

I mapped the journey of a worker with a complaint before writing any code, then built a web app on a free-tier Supabase database with two faces: a phone-first form for workers and a desktop console for HR.

## Routing by severity

Not every complaint deserves the same clock. I read through historical Red Book entries to find the most frequent grievance types, then designed a three-level matrix. Common issues are offered as quick options and categorised automatically; anything unusual goes to HR for manual categorisation.

## Built for the shop floor

Most of the design work was about removing reasons not to use it.

- **No app, no login friction.** A worker with any smartphone reaches the form in under 10 seconds from a QR code.
- **A photo counts as a complaint.** Some workers find typing hard, so they can write on paper, take a picture and upload it. HR reads the note in the dashboard.
- **A tracking ID and a chat thread.** Every complaint gets an ID the worker can check at any time, and a direct line to HR instead of silence.
- **Staying free.** Supabase's free tier has storage limits, so I added a one-click export that lets HR archive logs offline every month.

## What the pilot showed

<Callout>
  The paper process did not fail because officers were careless. It failed because it depended on memory and paper in a loud, fast plant.
</Callout>

Two things came out of the pilot. Workers often could not describe a machine fault or a safety hazard in words, which is why the **photo upload** became a core feature rather than an extra.

The second was about visibility. Under the old system, three departments could each complain about the same transport vendor and HR would see three unrelated incidents in three books. In one database, those complaints showed up together as a single, actionable problem.
