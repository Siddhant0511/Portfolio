## The problem

The Time Office at Alwar captures attendance on **ZKTeco** biometric devices, managed through **Easy WDMS**. That data then has to sync to the company's central **CLMS** platform, where payroll is calculated.

Capture worked. Transmission did not. The sync failed intermittently, with partial transfers and corrupted records, and the failures clustered on the **25th and 26th of every month**, exactly when attendance closes for payroll.

A second problem sat on top of the first. The process allowed informal manual overrides, and late arrivals used them freely, with nobody clearly accountable. Together, the two issues left the Time Office re-checking and re-entering attendance by hand during the most sensitive days of the month, with payroll errors as the risk.

## Finding the root causes

The failures were not random. They lined up with peak-volume days, which pointed at load rather than faulty hardware. Working through the stack layer by layer isolated three causes, in order of impact.

<Tiers
  scaleLabel="Impact"
  items={[
    {
      kicker: 'Layer 1, Primary',
      level: 3,
      title: 'Server concurrency',
      body: 'Every Ashok Leyland plant pushed its bulk attendance to CLMS at the same time, and the server could not process them in parallel.',
      meta: 'Dropped packets, mid-transfer timeouts',
    },
    {
      kicker: 'Layer 2, Secondary',
      level: 2,
      title: 'Device IP misalignment',
      body: 'ZKTeco devices used dynamic IP addresses. When an address changed, the link to WDMS broke without any local warning.',
      meta: 'Silent sync failures',
    },
    {
      kicker: 'Layer 3, Tertiary',
      level: 1,
      title: 'Firewall throttling',
      body: 'The firewall read high-volume traffic on ports 8080 and 443 as anomalous and rejected it.',
      meta: 'Valid attendance packets blocked',
    },
  ]}
/>

## Fixing it without a budget

I ranked the options by cost and by which layer they addressed. Three of the four fixes cost nothing or very little; the expensive one became a fallback, not the plan.

<DataTable
  caption="Technical solution matrix"
  head={['Solution', 'Cost and timing', 'Layer addressed', 'Feasibility']}
  highlightRow={0}
  rows={[
    ['Staggered sync schedules', 'Zero cost, immediate', 'Layer 1, concurrency', 'High'],

    ['CLMS server RAM and CPU upgrade', 'High cost, long term', 'Layer 1, backup option', 'Medium'],

The primary fix was a schedule, not a server. Instead of every plant pushing data at once, each location gets its own 30-minute sync window.

## Fixing the behaviour

Technology alone would not stop the manual overrides. The policy side made accountability personal again.

<Steps
  items={[
    { title: 'Biometrics first', body: 'Biometric capture becomes the non-negotiable record of attendance, not one option among several.' },
    { title: 'Gate bypass slips', body: 'A late arrival needs a physical, authorised slip issued at the security gate, hard-capped at three per worker per month.' },
    { title: 'Worker self-marking', body: 'In exceptional cases the worker signs their own attendance, so line managers supervise rather than do data entry.' },
  ]}
/>
