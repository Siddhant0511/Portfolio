## The problem

Before a tyre goes on a truck at Alwar, an operator reads the markings on its sidewall and checks them against a paper specification for that vehicle. The line keeps moving while they do it.

That check depended on experience and attention, and both run out. Wrong tyres got fitted: the wrong size, the wrong brand, or the wrong load index on an axle. When a mismatch was caught late, the vehicle needed rework and the line lost time.

## What the system had to do

**AL-TICS**, the Ashok Leyland Tyre Identification and Classification System, was designed around four requirements:

- **No paper.** Specifications appear on screen for the vehicle at the station.
- **Near-zero latency, fully local.** No cloud calls, no API bills, no dependence on plant internet.
- **Impossible to skip.** Operators under time pressure must not be able to bypass the scan.
- **A permanent record.** Every fitment logged against the vehicle's Chassis Body Number (CBN).

## Why a custom model

Tyre markings are not printed; they are **embossed into black rubber**. There is no colour contrast at all. Legibility depends on depth, shadow and the angle of the light, and on a factory floor there is dust too. Off-the-shelf OCR could not reach the accuracy a zero-defect line needs.

I also evaluated a local large language model and ruled it out. On the low-cost edge PCs available on the shop floor, inference was far too slow for a moving line. A **convolutional neural network** was the right tool: it learns edges, textures and embossing patterns directly from images and classifies a sidewall against the database of approved tyres.

## Designing out the shortcut

The biggest risk was not the model. It was an operator under pressure scanning anything to keep the line moving. So compliance was built into the physical workflow.

<Tiers
  cols={2}
  items={[

      meta: 'Digital handshake',

When the model detects the wrong tyre, AL-TICS stops being a checker and starts intervening.

AL-TICS supports the plant's zero-defect goals by moving verification from detection to prevention. And because every scan is logged, a future quality issue can be traced back to exactly which tyre went on which chassis, with a photo to prove it.
