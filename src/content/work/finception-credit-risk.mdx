## The brief

India's digital lending market has grown fast by reaching small-ticket and new-to-credit borrowers. That growth brings real vulnerabilities: unstable incomes, thin or missing bureau files, uneven verification, and rising regulatory scrutiny of unsecured lending.

The competition handed us a loan-level book and a simple question dressed up as a hard one: **where is this portfolio actually losing money, and what should the lender change?**

A 5.43% default rate looks moderate. The point of the analysis was to show it is an average over very different books: most segments are fine, and a few are badly underpriced.

## Risk 1: purpose mispricing

The sharpest finding came from putting each loan purpose's default rate next to the interest it earns. For a healthy segment, interest sits comfortably above default. For three purposes, it does not.

<Callout>Educational loans defaulted at 17.18% while charging 11.65% interest. The segment loses money before any operating cost.</Callout>

- **Educational:** 17.18% default against 11.65% interest; the team's expected-loss view put its net yield at about 1.34%
- **Small business:** 14.21% default against 16.15% interest, almost no margin for loss given default or costs
- **Wedding:** 11.62% default overall, rising to **22.37%** for ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 loans, a red-flag segment

## Risk 2: small-ticket concentration

88% of the book sits in loans under ₹30,000. That is the core of digital lending's inclusion story, and also its volatility: small loans go to cash-flow-sensitive borrowers, and the riskiest purposes cluster in exactly these ranges.

The heatmap makes the second insight visible: risk is not a property of purpose or size alone, but of **specific purpose and size combinations**. Some of the worst cells rest on small numbers of loans, which is itself a reason for caution when pricing them.

## Risk 3: grade and tenor tail risk

Credit grades did their job, sharply. Grade G loans defaulted nine times as often as grade A. Longer tenors added risk on top: lenders stretch tenors to make EMIs affordable, and lifetime default probability rises with them.
