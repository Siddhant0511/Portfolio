## The brief

For the Fintech course, our group built **TezCredit**: a working, clickable prototype of how an Indian digital lender gives someone an unsecured personal loan of ₹1.5 lakh, fully online, in about five minutes. No branch, no paperwork.

Any lender has to do four jobs: prove who you are, check whether you can repay, decide and price the loan, and move the money. TezCredit does all four on top of India's public digital infrastructure. Each of the five team members owned one layer of the product: the journey, the experience, the integrations, the credit decision and compliance.

The prototype runs in the browser. Everything below comes from the real screens; no real Aadhaar, bank, bureau or money is involved.

## The decision engine

This is where a lender makes or loses money. The engine rests on two ideas: the **credit score**, and **FOIR**, the fixed obligations to income ratio: all of a borrower's EMIs, including the new one, divided by their income.

The policy ceiling is 50%. The engine does not simply say yes or no; if the full amount would push FOIR past 50%, it offers the largest amount that keeps the borrower inside the line.

Pricing follows risk: a safer borrower gets a lower rate from the same lender panel, because the price of a loan is really the price of its risk. Every decision screen shows the reasons, score, FOIR and verified income, so there is no black box.

## Designed for real Indian users

- **One step at a time.** Each screen says exactly what to do next, such as "Finish KYC".
- **Honest numbers.** The safe-to-borrow figure stays greyed out until identity is verified: no flashy "you've won ₹5 lakh".
- **Language and access.** The whole app switches to Hindi, Tamil, Telugu or Malayalam, and runs comfortably on a low-cost phone.
- **Plain-language permissions.** Settings explain why a lender would ever ask for camera or location access.

The takeaway from building it: modern lending in India is not magic. It is public digital infrastructure, sound credit judgement and strict consumer protection, stitched into one calm screen.
