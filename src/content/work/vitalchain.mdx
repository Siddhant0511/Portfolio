## The problem

India's vital records, the births, marriages and deaths that decide who gets an identity, a pension or an inheritance, still live largely on paper, in separate offices, under the control of individual clerks.

The team's opening scenario made it concrete. A 78-year-old pensioner dies in a Delhi hospital. His family bribes a clerk ₹5,000 to delay the death certificate and keeps drawing his ₹15,000 monthly pension for three years. By the time an audit catches it, ₹5.4 lakh is gone and nobody is accountable. One bribe, one missing file, and the system fails.

## What VitalChain does

We built a working prototype, in a single HTML file, that puts vital records on a hash chain shared by the authorities that need them. It uses the browser's real **Web Crypto SHA-256** and a real proof-of-work mining step; only the peer-to-peer network is simulated inside the page.

### The avalanche effect

In the Hash Lab, changing a single letter of a birth record, "Kumar" to "Kunar", changes 60 of the 64 characters in its hash. There is no such thing as a small edit. And because hashing only works one way, seeing a hash does not reveal the certificate behind it.

<Gallery variant="single" zoom images={[{ src: hashLab, alt: 'Hash Lab: one letter changed in a birth record changes 60 of 64 hash characters' }]} />

### Breaking the chain

After issuing a birth, a marriage and a death certificate, we edit the name on the birth record to "FAKE PERSON". The block is flagged as tampered immediately, and every later block breaks, because each one still points at the original hash.

<Gallery variant="single" zoom images={[{ src: tampered, alt: 'Certificate registry with Block 1 flagged as tampered after its name was changed to FAKE PERSON' }]} />

### Majority rules

A single copy can still be altered by whoever holds it. So the network gives three authorities their own copy. When the hospital's copy is tampered with, the Registrar and UIDAI still hold the original, and the vote rejects the fake: two honest nodes against one.

<Gallery variant="single" zoom images={[{ src: consensus, alt: 'Nodes and consensus view: hospital node tampered, registrar and UIDAI honest, consensus vote rejects the fake record' }]} />

<Callout>These are not technology problems. They are trust problems, and a blockchain is a way to spread trust across institutions.</Callout>
